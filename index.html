Rebuilding e-commerce marketplace for South Asian creatives

Bengali art marketplace — a buyer site, an artist Seller Studio and an admin console, rebuilt in four weeks from a template shop with test data in its filters.

Lead AI Engineer and Product Designer Oct 20 – Nov 18, 2026
  • In four weeks Chitrok went from a template shop to a clickable marketplace: 24 desktop pages, 31 phone screens, 57 real works catalogued and 3 connected products — buyer site, Seller Studio and admin console.
  • Led product and design and directed Claude (AI build assistant): MVP scope, information architecture, flows, components, states, motion, accessibility, AI direction and review, UI kit upkeep and developer handoff. Business owner Sohel Ozzaman owned catalogue, policies, image permissions and payment/shipping decisions.
  • Fixed the old site’s dead ends: a visible filter rail with real tags only, a cart drawer that never covers the art, view-in-a-room for scale, audited links, and accounts with guest checkout and order tracking.
  • Cut order tracking from 5 clicks to 1 in the Nov 16 scripted usability audit, with a phone screen for every page.
Read the full case study (PDF) 6 pages · Bijoya D. · Oct 20 – Nov 18, 2026

Inside the case study

My part was the edits: which output shipped, what changed and why. Claude drafted fast; I decided what shipped, reviewing each screen and pinning comments to the exact spot.

The problem — the old chitrok.com

  • Stock template with test data in the filters (“test”, “dev2025”, “dev100”) and one result for a whole page.
  • Mega menu hid the catalogue; the cart flyout covered the art; there was no sense of scale.
  • Dead links and error pages; buyers pushed off-site to social links; no accounts or order tracking.

The fixes

  • Visible filter rail with counts, shape tiles and price range — only real tags.
  • Cart drawer slides in from the edge, so the grid stays visible.
  • Hover any work to see it in a room; sizes in cm and inches.
  • Every link audited; empty categories say “Coming soon”.
  • No outside social links — messages and commissions stay on Chitrok.
  • Accounts, guest checkout, order tracking and stamp receipts.

Signature moments — only Chitrok’s

  • Sentence-builder search: “I’m seeking any kind of art in any style that’s within my budget.”
  • Arched category frames like gallery niches, on a stamp wall.
  • Confetti on add-to-cart in clay, gold and sage.
  • My collection: each purchase becomes a postage stamp that opens its order, certificate and story.
  • Receipts as postage stamps with a Dhaka postmark.

Familiar first, novel second (Jakob’s Law, the von Restorff effect): cart top-right, heart on the card, filters on the left, two-column checkout. Influences were borrowed only where they solved a problem — eBay (seller trust signals; step order status Paid → Packing → Shipped → In transit → Delivered), Saatchi Art (view in a room, artist-first pages, certificate of authenticity) and Etsy (maker stories, favourites, a forgiving cart).

Interaction & motion

Every control has hover, pressed, selected, focus, disabled and error states — always two cues, never colour alone. Room-view fade .25s · cart press spring .42s · confetti .95s · drawer slide-in .28s. With prefers-reduced-motion, every transition stops and confetti never renders. Motion confirms an action; it never carries meaning on its own.

Colour, type & accessibility

Action green#3E6B48 Off-white ground#FAF7F1 White cards#FFFFFF Brown#2E2118 Amber#E8A33D Clay red#9A2C17

One green for every action, so the paintings bring the colour. Arial-family sans for headings. Every text pair passes WCAG AA — green on white 6.2:1, body text 15.2:1. 44px touch targets (52px on phone), alt text that describes the art, and honest placeholders: unknown facts show as [Medium to confirm], never invented.

Her hand in it — directing the AI output

  • Kept the filter and tag system always visible instead of tucked into menus.
  • Put medium, size and orientation under the product title; details and shipping moved into drawers.
  • Removed message buttons and outside social links; reordered artist pages — cover, name, Commission and Follow, then works.
  • Round cart icons with confetti instead of text “Add to cart” buttons on dense grids.
  • Cut 15 thin medium pages down to one empty-gallery template.
  • Replaced a sign-up wall with guest checkout, and a plain email receipt with stamp receipts.

BrewBase — Café Discovery Platform

Café discovery startup, Toronto — find a café for what you plan to do and how you want the space to feel, not just where you are.

Co-Founder & AI Product Designer (CEO) 2025 – Present
  • Co-founded a café discovery startup and leads its 4-person team: 1 product designer and 2 full-stack developers.
  • Discovery starts from intent, not just location: BrewBase helps someone find a café that suits what they plan to do and how they want the space to feel. Home opens with “What are you looking for?” and six plan-based prompts, each opening Discover with a prefilled query.
  • Designed the full journey end to end — synced card, list, and map discovery; editorial café profiles with practical attributes, honest unknown states, and provenance; saving into collections; and exporting a saved list via “Take these out” (CSV, copy or print) — a list export, not food ordering.
  • Built an evidence model for café facts: every attribute carries an explicit status — verified, venue-reported, estimated, or unknown — so missing data is never presented as a venue attribute.
  • Authored the complete handoff package (October 2026) — product blueprint, design handoff, frontend handoff, and data & asset documentation — alongside a working static prototype.
  • Conducting user interviews and usability tests to refine and prioritize, managing roadmap and design handoff, and preparing for a planned Toronto Tech Week 2027 debut.
  • Hand-drew the entire brand illustration system herself — every BrewBase illustration and vector, from the café icon patterns and postcard to the mugs, latte art and loading animation in the gallery below, is her own drawing.
Try the live prototype Full BrewBase illustration set (Canva) Live prototype: 6 illustrative cafés — discovery, café profiles, saved collections and Gatherings. All BrewBase illustrations and vectors were hand-drawn by Bijoya herself.

Inside the prototype — from the October 2026 handoff

Start from a plan — the six home prompts

Find a corner to read Study group Table for two Work from a coffee shop Plan a café crawl A third place for my friends

Flow

  1. Home
    2. →
  2. Discover
    3. →
  3. Café profile
    4. →
  4. Save & collections
    5. →
  5. Take these out — export list

Discover combines search, 8 quick filters, neighbourhood and sort across Cards, List and Map views that stay in sync, over 6 illustrative cafés. Café profiles show the practical details that decide a visit — Wi-Fi, outlets, seating, noise, laptop policy, accessibility, transit/parking, hours and a signature order — with anything unknown shown honestly as “Unknown”. Saved “field notes” collections can be renamed, and “Take these out” exports a saved list (Download CSV, Copy list or Print) — it is not food ordering. Gatherings adds café events such as a silent reading hour, sketch club and vinyl listening night, linked to their cafés, and a profile / preferences surface rounds out the product.

Design principles

Start with the visitor’s intent. Make café information scannable and comparable. Distinguish verified facts from unknown or illustrative information — never imply a venue attribute from missing data.

Visual system

Paper#F5EFE3 Paper Light#FCF9F2 Espresso#38251D Ink#2D251F Cobalt#293B9B Sage#73816A Terracotta#B45E3F

DM Serif Display for editorial headlines and café names · DM Sans for product UI · Kalam for handwritten accents.

Build status: working static prototype in vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript — no backend yet. The prototype covers discovery, café profiles, saved collections and Gatherings; it has no ratings, stars or reviews, and no ordering — ratings and reviews are planned for a later phase. The café content in the prototype is fictional, illustrative demo data (6 cafés), and is labelled as such in the product.

MyManagement Portal – BBA Students Central Hub for Resources, Career and Program Pathways

University of Toronto Scarborough · Department of Management

MyManagement is a U of T student portal for BBA students — a place to navigate their way through nine different streams, find jobs, and build their careers.

Design Lead · UX/UI Designer 2021 – 2022 · 4 months · Team of 2 designers
  • Secured $35,000 in department investment for web developers and 2 new UX/UI roles, after 4 months of solo user research and a full working WordPress prototype.
  • As Design Lead in a 2-designer team, co-led the product design from concept to implementation — user experience, visual system, illustrations and design documentation.
  • Co-led design direction, UX strategy, content planning, and user flows for a centralized platform unifying events, activity management, and experiential learning for 18+ student clubs and ~2,000 Management students.
  • Developed a centralized UI design system, information architecture, wireframes, navigation concepts, and responsive page structures.
  • Ran research and user testing with stakeholders, and produced design guidance and wayfinding assets.
Web UI screens (PDF) MyManagement style guide (PDF) Early WordPress prototype Web UI: 2 pages · Style guide: 12 pages — logo, Open Sans typography, colour palettes, graphic style and widget styles.

Inside the case study

The opportunity: students were navigating multiple platforms to reach important resources — fragmented information, an inconsistent experience and limited engagement, leading to confusion and missed opportunities. MyManagement brings it together in one place.

Process — five steps

  1. Discover — research student needs & pain points
    2. →
  2. Define — identify key opportunities
    3. →
  3. Structure — build IA & user flows
    4. →
  4. Design — wireframes, visual design & illustrations
    5. →
  5. Deliver — document the system for implementation

Information architecture — six sections

Content is organized around the core needs of the Management student journey: Management School (programs, advising, news) · Management Clubs (clubs, events, updates) · Student Services (appointments, support, resources) · Academic Pathways (career, co-op, internships) · Academic Resources (guides, templates, policies) · Alumni (network, benefits, events). A modular dashboard gives students quick access to what matters most — Dashboard, Academic Resources, and Case Competitions & Conferences are the key screens.

Visual system & illustration

A clean, trustworthy and approachable identity in the Management brand: Open Sans (Bold / Regular) typography, a navy-and-teal palette with warm accent colours, and custom flat illustrations that bring warmth and clarity to complex information — documented in full in the style guide above.

Accessibility & standards

  • Designed with AODA compliance in mind.
  • Accessible colour contrast and consistent, readable typography.
  • Alt text for all artwork and logos.
  • Keyboard-navigable components.

From prototype to portal

The work began as a very early WordPress prototype (linked above) built during solo research, then grew through user-flow and wireframe iterations for pages like Case Competitions & Conferences — mapping objectives, actions and visuals, structuring events by Management stream, and flagging issues found along the way, such as event labels that made filtering hard — into the final MyManagement screens in the gallery below.
