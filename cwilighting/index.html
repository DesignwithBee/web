The core idea

People are everything here.

CWI has thrived for 25+ years on relationships, trade shows, and craftsmanship — reps who know their retailers, retailers who know their clients by name. That human network is the brand’s luxury: not distance, but care, craft, and trust you can feel in a room.

The digital job is not to replace that, and not to change the brand. It’s to show what CWI has already been doing — the hands that make the glass, the rooms the light transforms, the dealers who install it — so that someone who discovers CWI cold, online, feels the same warmth as someone who was introduced by a person they trust.

Keep the luxury. Put the people back in the frame. Make the network visible.

2 The dealer network is the strength

Most brands have to build distribution. CWI already has it — and dealers are already creating content.

  • 745 doors is distribution most competitors would envy. The groundwork — relationships, showrooms, trade presence at Dallas and High Point — is done. Digital doesn't need to create demand from zero; it needs to make this network easy to find and easy to share.
  • Dealers are already posting CWI. Moko Home (Perth, Ontario) has posted CWI collection reels, and other retailers merchandise large CWI catalogues online. That is proof the product photographs and sells in real rooms.
  • The assets already exist. Product pages carry 3D View, the Resources page holds the 2025 Catalog and January 2026 Supplement as flip-books, and virtual showroom tours for Dallas and High Point are live on the site.

Sources: Moko Home reel, Nov 2, 2026 · Arcus product page (3D View) · Resources page · Virtual Showrooms page

3 Channel read

The rundown, channel by channel, in bullet points — ordered by where the funnel actually starts. Instagram, Pinterest and LinkedIn are the main channels, with real life — showrooms and trade shows — feeding the same path, and all of it pointing back to the Shopify website. Facebook, TikTok and YouTube sit at a lower tier as mirrors. Nothing here needs a rebrand — it needs consistency, people, and a cadence, all within the existing brand system. Each channel closes with its potential threat and its opportunity, highlighted.

Main channels — Instagram · Pinterest · LinkedIn → Shopify website
Instagram — flagged for attention
9,080 followers
  • About 2 posts a week — Reels and carousels dominate the feed.
  • Content reads as AI-generated — polished, people-free product visuals, with little of the makers, rooms, and people behind the light.
  • Visible engagement is low — roughly 0.1–0.3% per post (about 8–53 likes, 0–4 comments on sampled posts).
  • That low engagement sits oddly against the scale behind it — a network of 745 retailer locations and active trade shows at Dallas Market Center and High Point, North Carolina.
  • Highlights are well organised — HPMKT ’26, 2026 Collection, Dallas 2026, Meets, Custom Lighting.
  • Bio carries five separate links, and two of them are stale as of November 27, 2026.

Source: instagram.com/cwilighting — grid & bio reviewed Nov 27, 2026. Engagement counts from sampled posts via search index. Retailer count from Local Dealers page, counted Nov 27, 2026.

Potential threatBroken links in the bio are a dead end at the moment of highest intent — five links, two of them stale, so a customer who was ready to act hits a broken path and leaves. AI-looking, people-free content plus quiet conversation next to a 745-door network and active trade shows also makes the brand look smaller and less human than it is, and stale links tax the luxury polish.

Opportunity — how to improve itPut people back in the frame: real makers, real rooms, dealer installs, and trade-show moments, cut as Reels and carousels that invite conversation, not just reach. Replace the five bio links with one clean link hub — a single Carrd.co or Beacons.ai–style card holding every current link in one place, kept fresh — and make the grid work harder: when a post showcases a product, its entry in the hub links straight through to that product’s Shopify page, so the path runs post → product → retailer without hunting.

4 Customer journey: Reel to retailer

Walked live on Nov 27, 2026, from a single entry point: an Instagram Reel reaches the right person — for example, someone opening a café, restaurant, or showroom — and they try to get from a light they love to the retailer who sells it. It took 14 clicks to reach Get Directions.

Clicks 1–2

Reel → profile → website

The Reel works — it reaches the right person. On landing, there are many links to parse and two were dead, so the first decision is harder than it should be.

Clicks 3–6

Home → categories → products

Featured Collections land beautifully — Arcus, Petra, Contour, Curva read as luxury. From there, categories (flush mounts, mini pendants, outdoor) lead into the product grid.

Clicks 7–8

Browsing with no way to narrow down

No filters on the product grid, so discovery means opening products one by one. A liked product had no second image and no in-room view, and its sold-out status only appeared after clicking in — it should be flagged on the listing itself.

Clicks 9–14

Where to Buy → Local Dealers → Directions

The dealer list requires extensive scrolling, the map did not work in the walkthrough, and there was no link out to the retailer’s own website. Click 14 finally reaches Get Directions — the right ending, after a long middle.

Source: live walkthrough, Nov 27, 2026, starting at instagram.com/cwilighting through Local Dealers page. Dead links, filter, image, sold-out, and map observations are from that walkthrough.

In one line

“I could have found out a lot more information in the meanwhile.” The fix is not more content — it’s a seamless path from the Reel to the product, to the retailer, to the right contact.

Potential threats — what’s at stake if the friction stays

The Reel does its job — it creates desire and sends the right person to the site. Every threat below sits after that moment, in the stretch where desire either becomes a showroom visit or quietly cools.

  • The sale leaks at the moment of highest intent. A customer who hits dead links, browses with no way to filter, discovers a product is sold out only after clicking in, and then faces a long dealer list with a map that doesn’t work, rarely complains — they simply buy the light they could reach, from whoever made it easiest. The competitor risk is not a better product; it’s a shorter path.
  • The retailer loses a sale the brand already generated. CWI’s content and collections create the demand; the dealer is meant to close it. Friction in the Where to Buy stretch means the retailer never meets a customer who was already sold — the network’s greatest strength, 745 doors, becomes the hardest part of the journey to use.
  • Small breakages tax luxury perception. For a premium brand, polish is the promise: two dead links on landing and a broken map don’t read as technical glitches, they read as carelessness — the opposite of the craftsmanship the product itself proves in person.
  • Disappointment lands on the product, not the path. A sold-out surprise after clicking in, or a liked product with no second image and no in-room view, attaches frustration to the fixture itself — when the fixture was never the problem.

The encouraging read: every one of these is a path fix, not a brand fix — the fixes are mapped in Section 5, Opportunities, inside the existing luxury positioning and brand manual.

1

Put people back in the frame — luxuriously

The craft story is CWI’s differentiator: the Nov 9 Petra post (“from the first sketch to the finished piece”) and the torch-on-glass hero film prove it. Make it a system: a recurring Sketch → Glass → Glow series, behind-the-scenes of how glass is made, and B-roll of lights living in real places — homes, restaurants, bars, retailer installs, and hospitality spaces. Real people, real rooms, shot to the manual’s standard. Polished, people-free product video alone can read as generated; people are what make luxury feel human.

Sources: Instagram — Nov 9 Petra post · Homepage hero film

2

Shorten the Reel-to-retailer path

Fix the journey in Section 4, Customer journey without touching the catalogue model: product filters, sold-out flagged on the listing, a second image / in-room view / 3D cue on each product, a working embedded dealer map, and links out to each retailer’s website. Fewer clicks, same luxury — just less friction between desire and a showroom visit.

Sources: Local Dealers · Where to Buy

Where I fit in

I’ve already done this exact fix on a live marketplace. At Chitrok, task-based usability audits cut click navigation 66% — sales tracking went from 6 clicks to 2 — and I structured a 57-artwork catalogue into 7 filters, replacing 15 thin category pages with one filter model, at WCAG AA standard. This review’s 14-click walkthrough is the same method. And because CWI stays luxury, the fix is path clarity — filters, flags, maps, links — never public pricing.

3

Connect the reps’ catalogue app back to the website

Sales reps already showcase products in a catalogue app — a curated, in-person experience. Tie that experience back to the site: the same product storytelling, imagery, and collection order in both places; deep links / QR from the app experience to the matching product page and Where to Buy; and web analytics that show which app-showcased products people explore afterwards. One luxury story, whether a rep is in the room or the customer is browsing alone at midnight. CWI already builds its Shopify presence with Claude — that same build workflow makes this kind of app-to-site consistency fast to iterate, inside the manual.

4

Systematise dealer content into “Installed By”

Dealers are already posting. Give them a named, repeatable feature — a monthly Installed By series — plus a simple on-brand asset kit (catalogue flip-books, spec sheets, 3D links already exist on Resources) so showrooms can post beautifully and consistently, and the main account can reshare it. It turns 745 locations into a content network.

Sources: Moko Home reel · Resources page

5

Short, saveable films — “one fixture, five moods” in a single real room, polls (“Which lighting makes this room feel better?”), day-in-the-life and trade-show behind-the-scenes — sell a spec-sheet feature emotionally, and they invite the comments the feed currently doesn’t get.

Source: Arcus product page (3D View, 2700K–5000K)

Reactivate the quiet channels — no new shoots needed

And close the loop on every launch: each supplement drop gets one coordinated asset set across Instagram, email, homepage Featured Collections, and Highlights — plus designed, infographic-style reporting on reach and trade-show results that people actually read.
